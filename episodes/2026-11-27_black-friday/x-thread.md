1/ Happy Black Friday. You've probably heard the name means stores finally get "in the black." That story came later. The name came from the police.
2/ Philadelphia, early 1960s: on the day after Thanksgiving, suburban shoppers and tourists poured downtown. In some years the Army-Navy game was that Saturday too.
3/ Police dealt with traffic jams, accidents and shoplifting, and worked longer shifts. Their name for the day: Black Friday.
4/ A Dec. 1961 trade newsletter (Public Relations News) noted Philadelphia officers called the post-Thanksgiving days "Black Friday and Black Saturday."
5/ Merchants didn't like it and pushed a friendlier name: "Big Friday." It didn't stick.
6/ By the late 1980s the term was used nationwide, and the red-ink-to-black-ink profit story caught on.
7/ Bonus: before shopping, "Black Friday" meant a market disaster. On Sept. 24, 1869, Jay Gould and Jim Fisk's scheme to corner the gold market collapsed.
