1/ Flying home this weekend? If an officer wipes your hands with a little cloth, here's what that swab is actually doing.

2/ The idea: anyone who handles explosives can leave invisible traces on what they touch. The swab collects them from hands and bags.

3/ Inside the trace detector, the swab is heated so any residue turns to vapor.

4/ Then the molecules get an electric charge, usually from a small radioactive source (nickel-63).

5/ An electric field pulls the charged molecules down a tube against a flow of gas. Each kind crosses at its own speed, in milliseconds.

6/ Arrival time is the fingerprint. The machine compares it with a library of explosives: a strong match alarms, no match doesn't.

7/ Different molecules can share similar drift times, which is why experts have long looked to mass spectrometry to improve airport trace detection.

Sources: NIST IR 7240; DHS S&T (2022); National Academies (2004). Video: @bonusbraincell
