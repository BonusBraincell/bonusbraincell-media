1/ Why can one narrow strait half a world away move your gas price? The U.S. got only 8% of its 2025 crude imports from the Middle East Gulf (EIA).

2/ But the Strait of Hormuz carried about 20 million barrels of oil a day in 2024. That's roughly a fifth of global petroleum use (EIA).

3/ If that flow were choked, spare pipelines could reroute only a few million barrels a day. EIA estimated ~2.6 million; IEA's 2026 factsheet says 3.5-5.5 million of capacity.

4/ 84% of the crude and condensate through the strait went to Asia in 2024. Lose those barrels and buyers look elsewhere.

5/ EIA: "Crude oil is traded in a global market," and prices of crude streams "tend to move closely together." So U.S. crude tends to rise too.

6/ Crude is the biggest piece of a gallon of gasoline: about half of the 2025 average retail price (EIA).
